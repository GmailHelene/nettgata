# Customer needs, pain points, churn and decision criteria for new websites / webshops (2025-2026), split by customer size

Scope note: research date 2026-10-31. Geography is flagged on every figure: NO = Norway, EU/Nordic, US, Global. Almost all hard survey numbers on "what small businesses want from a website" are US (Clutch, BrightLocal, All About Cookies, GoDaddy multi-country). Norway-specific survey data on website needs is very thin (see Gaps). Vendor-published surveys (Squarespace, Clutch, BrightLocal, Hostinger, Netconomy, Patchstack, MACH Alliance) are directional; each is marked where it matters.

## 1. Top priorities (price, ease of use, design, mobile, speed, Google, AI search, bookings, payments, support, ownership, security, time to launch) and how they differ by size

### Takeaway
No single survey ranks all of these criteria against each other. Triangulating: micro/small owners buy a website to get sales and credibility, now say cost is rarely the barrier, and invest next in redesign, speed, mobile, content/SEO and security. Visibility in Google AND in AI answers is now a stated top priority for roughly two thirds to three quarters of SMBs, but most do not know how to act on it. Mid-market/enterprise criteria shift to integration (ERP/PIM/CRM), self-service/pricing, SLA/security and architecture flexibility.

### Cited Findings

**Solo / micro / small (US unless noted)**
- 83% of US small businesses have a website (64% in 2018; 12% launched within the past year). Reasons for investing in a website: increase sales/revenue 39%, build brand awareness/credibility 24%, provide information/customer support 22%. Survey of 406 US small-business owners, 5 Aug 2025. — [Clutch, State of Small Business Websites 2025](https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025) (published 2025-08-21)
- Planned website investments next 12 months (90% plan to invest): full redesign/visual refresh 58%, speed/performance 53%, mobile experience 45%, content marketing 38%, SEO 37%, security updates 34%, adding ecommerce/payments 32%. Same Clutch survey (US, Aug 2025). — [Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025)
- Cost is no longer the main reason for not having a site: only 16% of businesses without a website cite cost (26% in 2018); 34% say a website is "not relevant to my industry". US, 2025. — [Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025)
- SEO is the top lead source for 40% of small businesses with a website; 40% of those without a site rely on referrals; social media is top source for only 10%. US, 2025. — [Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025)
- 61% of small businesses with a website update it at least weekly (hours, promotions, blog, bug fixes). US, 2025. — [Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025)
- When picking an external tech partner, small businesses rank quality of work first (27%) and cost second (23%); only 39% prioritise web development spending (app dev 38%, software 37%, AI 32%). US, Clutch Dec 2025. — [Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/invest-tech-meet-customer-expectations)
- Shopper-side friction (Clutch 2025 consumer survey, US): 29% of online shoppers name slow-loading sites among top frustrations, 19% say complicated checkouts drive them away. — [Clutch, State of Web Design](https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-web-design) (2025-08-20)
- Microbusiness owners' top barriers to success: lack of financial support/high costs 54%, lack of guidance 38%, lack of expertise/skills 36%, lack of time 33% (GoDaddy Small Business Research Lab 2025 annual report; multi-country national surveys in AU/CA/UK/US, 2025; the extraction did not isolate which country each barrier figure belongs to). — [GoDaddy Annual Report 2025](https://godaddy.com/research/explore-the-data/annual-report-2025) (2025-12-01)
- Of GoDaddy's microbusiness owners, the share naming their own website as the main place customers can buy: US 56%, AU 42%, CA 40%, UK 30% (UK: 32% say social media is the primary way to attract customers, only 19% sell directly on social). For owners who do not sell on their site, 73% (US), 69% (CA), 65% (AU), 59% (UK) say it is critical for marketing and credibility. 2025. — [GoDaddy](https://godaddy.com/research/explore-the-data/annual-report-2025)
- AI use by microbusiness owners 2025: US 49% (about 25% in early 2024), AU 46%, CA 38%, UK 35% (27% in 2024); top use is writing content. — [GoDaddy](https://godaddy.com/research/explore-the-data/annual-report-2025)
- US SMB owners (n>1,000, 2026): 98% are primarily responsible for their own marketing decisions; 28% say marketing "feels too technical"; 25% think marketing won't make a real difference. — [BrightLocal SMB Marketing Report 2026](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/smb-marketing-report/)

**Local discovery, Google Business Profile, reviews (US, BrightLocal)**
- Top reasons consumers choose a local business (n=1,227 US local searchers, 2026): clear and complete information 32%, most/best reviews 30%, clear pricing or an offer 29%. — [Timpson Marketing citing BrightLocal channel/decision studies](https://timpsonmarketing.com/blog/do-customers-use-chatgpt-to-find-local-businesses) (2026-08-05) (secondary; BrightLocal primary pages not directly fetched for this figure)
- 85% of consumers consider contact info and opening hours an important factor when researching local businesses; 46% "very important". US 2025. — [BrightLocal Consumer Search Behavior](https://brightlocal.com/research/consumer-search-behavior) (2025-04-29)
- 97% of consumers read local reviews; 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews; 74% only care about reviews from the last 3 months; 31% will only use 4.5+ stars (17% a year earlier); 54% visit the business website after reading positive reviews (32% in 2019). n=1,002 US adults, Feb 2026. — [BrightLocal LCRS 2026](https://brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review) (2026-02-11) and [Sidekick Digital summary](https://sidekickdigital.com/blog/what-brightlocals-2026-local-consumer-review-survey-means-for-your-business)
- Supply-side gap: 85% of US SMBs say they own a Google Business Profile but only 35% keep it active, 21% have not updated it in 3+ months; 47% have fewer than 20 Google reviews; only 51% regularly reply to reviews; only 41% keep an active website and 26% are active on citation/directory sites. US SMB survey, press release 2026-10-22. — [BrightLocal via PR Newswire](http://prnewswire.com/news-releases/brightlocal-report-small-businesses-losing-online-visibility-as-social-and-ai-prioritised-over-google-302916401.html)
- Templated/generic review replies make 50% of consumers less likely to use a business (worse than no reply, 42%). — [BrightLocal LCRS 2026 via ReviewMankey](https://reviewmankey.com/blog/online-review-statistics-2026)

**AI search visibility (ChatGPT, Perplexity, AI Overviews)**
- 45% of US consumers used AI tools for local business recommendations in the past 12 months (6% a year earlier); ChatGPT 31%, Google AI Mode 23%; AI is now the 3rd most used source behind Google and Facebook. n=1,002, Feb 2026. — [BrightLocal AI trust](https://brightlocal.com/research/lcrs-ai-trust) (2026-03-10)
- But AI is a shortlisting step, not the closer: only 8% of local searchers began their latest local search inside an AI tool, only 2% used AI exclusively; 18% felt ready to contact the AI-named business, 75% go on to Google to verify; 88% of AI users fact-check. US, 2026. — [Timpson Marketing citing BrightLocal](https://timpsonmarketing.com/blog/do-customers-use-chatgpt-to-find-local-businesses); [BrightLocal AI visibility tool page](https://brightlocal.com/local-seo-tools/local-ai-visibility); [BrightLocal AI trust](https://brightlocal.com/research/lcrs-ai-trust)
- Business websites are 42% of citations in AI local answers and Google Business Profile 28.5% (BrightLocal, Aug 2026, 1,355 US/UK/AU locations, ~1.97M citations). — [SEOsandwitch compilation](https://seosandwitch.com/chatgpt-local-business-statistics) (2026-10-08); [BCC Interactive compilation](https://bccinteractive.com/blog/local-ai-search-statistics) (2026-10-10)
- SOCi 2026 Local Visibility Index (350,000+ locations, 2,751 multi-location brands): ChatGPT recommended 1.2% of locations, Gemini 11%, Perplexity 7.4%, vs 35.9% in Google's local 3-pack. — [BCC Interactive](https://bccinteractive.com/blog/local-ai-search-statistics)
- In one trades study ChatGPT named businesses with 1,000+ reviews 6.5x as often as those with fewer than 25; star rating made no difference (8ight.AI, Sep 2026, 39 trades x 49 cities; secondary reporting, treat as single-vendor study). — [SEOsandwitch](https://seosandwitch.com/chatgpt-local-business-statistics)
- Owner attitudes: Squarespace April 2026 survey (n=1,041 owners in US/CA/UK/AU, both Squarespace and non-Squarespace): 66% say AI search will be very/extremely important (over 4 in 10 say it already is); nearly 6 in 10 "don't know where to start"; more than half say their business is not optimised for AI search; over two thirds had not checked how they appear in AI answers, and 40% of those who checked found incorrect information; 34% had used no AI tools for marketing/SEO; 48% worry AI removes brand personality. — [Squarespace, The New Front Door](https://fr.squarespace.com/blog/ai-search-for-business-owners) (2026-07-07); [secondary summary](https://nocturnalmarketing.tech/blog/ai-search-small-business-website-guide) (2026-07-20)
- EMARKETER/Semify survey of SMB marketers (2026; sample size not stated in the snippet): 76.0% say appearing as a citation/recommendation in AI answers is extremely/very important; 38.7% report meaningful new traffic from AI tools in the past 12 months; 29.3% have had customers say they found them via AI; top challenge is measuring ROI of AI visibility (56.0%), ahead of time/expertise (42.7%) and budget (38.7%). — [EMARKETER](https://emarketer.com/content/smbs-recognize-ai-search-visibility-critical-struggle-measure-impact) (2026-10-26)
- AI is still small as a traffic source: Ahrefs (76,000 sites) estimates Google sends ~190x the referral traffic of ChatGPT (39.98% vs 0.21%); Semrush (50,000+ sites) put AI at 0.14% of site traffic in 2025 vs 16.04% organic search, though AI grew 66% in the year. — [Timpson Marketing citing Ahrefs/Semrush](https://timpsonmarketing.com/blog/do-customers-use-chatgpt-to-find-local-businesses) (secondary)

**Speed / mobile (global/US)**
- HTTP Archive Core Web Vitals (late 2025): share of sites passing Google's speed metrics Wix ~75%, Squarespace ~70%, average WordPress 46%; so "builders are slow" is no longer a safe claim. — [Francis Web Agency citing HTTP Archive](https://franciswebagency.com/insights/ai-website-builder-vs-custom-web-design) (2026-08-01). Consistent with another summary: Wix mobile CWV pass up from 55% to 74% YoY, Duda 85%, WordPress ~45% — [SDG Web Design](https://sdgwebdesign.com/blog/web-design-articles--bpc/website-builder-vs-web-design-agency--bp) (2026-09-13)

**Norway: consumer-side priorities for webshops**
- Vipps and BankID are described as near-obligatory in Norwegian checkout; Vipps has 4M+ users and is the most used and most preferred online payment method per PostNord E-commerce in the Nordics 2026; 60% of Norwegians shop online at least monthly (58% prior year). Norwegian webshops grew 13.9% to Oct 2025 (Virke). — [Shopify Norge, Hva er netthandel (citing PostNord, Virke)](https://shopify.com/no/blog/netthandel) (2026-05-26) (secondary)
- Practitioner/vendor claims (unverified, no primary survey): Vipps Hurtigkasse lifts completed mobile orders 25-45%; 23% abandon if preferred method is missing; 70%+ of Norwegian ecommerce is mobile. — [Nettpilot](https://nettpilot.no/hvilken-betalingslosning-bor-du-velge-til-din-nettbutikk) (2026-10-09)
- Norwegian platform pricing anchors (vendor self-reported): 24Nettbutikk/Mystore from ~400 kr/month incl. Klarna/Vipps/Norwegian shipping; 24Nettbutikk >1,000 stores, Mystore >1,600 stores; Shopify >8,500 Norwegian stores (BuiltWith 2024). — [Infoframe](https://infoframe.no/artikler/lage-nettbutikk) (2026-06-25); [Tennet](https://tennet.no/blogg/beste-nettbutikk-losning) (2026-01-01); [Inevo](https://inevo.no/blogg/shopify-netthandel) (2025-11-21)

**Mid-market / enterprise (B2B ecommerce buyers; global)**
- 900 C-suite/procurement leaders (Netconomy, published 2025-10-06; regions not listed): 83% say a high-quality self-service experience is crucial when choosing a new vendor; most valued features: price optimisation 67% and price transparency 47%, automated reordering 56%, integration with procurement systems 43% (large companies), real-time inventory visibility 42%; 43% still want a human sales rep for complex or expensive purchases. — [Netconomy](https://netconomy.net/blog/b2b-self-service-features)
- A 2024 Forrester-commissioned study (Zoovu) found 73% of B2B buyers expect the same digital experience as consumers. — [Shopify Norge B2B-guide citing the study](https://shopify.com/no/blog/saas-b2b-e-handel) (2026-03-26) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Price is a first-order filter for solo/micro (GoDaddy: cost is the #1 barrier at 54%), yet absent as the reason for no website (Clutch 16%). Reading: price decides "which tool/provider", while "relevance/credibility/leads" decides "whether at all". The product should sell outcomes (calls, bookings, credibility) rather than "cheap website".
- "Being found" has split into three layers that the product must cover: Google ranking, Google Business Profile/maps/reviews, and AI answer engines (which draw mostly on the business's own website, GBP, directories and reviews). Owners are anxious (two thirds say it matters) but unequipped (six in ten do not know where to start), which is a clear product gap: guided/automatic setup of structured data, consistent NAP, GBP and review prompts.
- Mobile and speed are hygiene factors, not differentiators, because major builders already pass CWV at 70-75%. Differentiation must come from ownership/portability, local fit (Vipps, Fiken, Bring/Posten, BankID, norsk support) and automation of post-launch work.
- For mid-market/enterprise the stated priorities are integration depth, price logic (customer-specific price lists), self-service reordering and procurement-system integration, not design.

### Gaps
- No survey found that ranks price vs ease of use vs design vs mobile vs speed vs support vs ownership vs security vs time-to-launch in a single instrument, for any size class. "Ownership/portability" and "support quality" are discussed qualitatively (Q2, Q4) but not ranked with percentages.
- No Norway-specific survey (Fiken, DNB, Innovasjon Norge, NHO, Virke, Digital Norway, Visma, SpareBank 1) with website feature priorities found; see Q7.
- Bookings as a stated priority: only product-mix data (Hostinger: appointment-based sites ~6% of builder sites) rather than owner-priority surveys.
- No AI-search data for Norway/Norwegian-language answers (all BrightLocal/SOCi/Squarespace figures are US or US/UK/AU/CA).

## 2. Pain points with current solutions

### Takeaway
The dominant pain clusters are (a) owners cannot finish or confidently run the site themselves (design/layout, security, custom functionality, time), (b) the site produces no traffic or leads, (c) cost creep and lock-in on closed builders, (d) maintenance/security burden on WordPress (plugin vulnerabilities, patch speed), and (e) agency dependency with slow, expensive small changes.

### Cited Findings

**DIY difficulty and time (US)**
- 1,000 US adults, May 2025: 32% have built a website; 74% of those used a builder service. Only about one quarter built without major issues or delays; top challenges: design/layout 37%, keeping the site safe and secure 31%, custom functionality 27%; 91% found some part overly difficult; 24% finished but it took much longer than expected. — [All About Cookies, Web Builder Statistics](https://allaboutcookies.org/web-builder-statistics) (2025-06-11)
- 51% of those who started a website project abandoned it before finishing (29% later finished with outside help, 22% never returned). Same survey. — [All About Cookies](https://allaboutcookies.org/web-builder-statistics)
- Unverified time-cost claim: "small business owners spend 11 hours per week managing their website (GoDaddy 2024)" appears only in an agency blog that did not link a primary page; not reproduced in GoDaddy's 2025 report that was fetched. Treat as unconfirmed. — [Web Guider blog](https://seashell-sparrow-408482.hostingersite.com/blog/hidden-costs-diy-websites) (2025-10-06)

**No traffic / no leads**
- Theo's audit of 5,974 small-business sites (self-selected: owners who ran the free audit; not random): 48% of 4,027 sites with search data got an estimated zero Google-search visitors per month, median site about 1; by platform: Shopify 50%, WordPress 35%, Wix 47%, Squarespace 49%, GoDaddy Website Builder 56%, Weebly/Square Online 52%. — [Theo, small business website mistakes](https://theo.site/blog/resources/small-business-website-mistakes) (2026-09-17)
- Practitioner view (not survey data): neglect is "friction plus uncertainty": fear of breaking the layout, decision fatigue, no clear next step, and "no payoff" (no calls/bookings), plus silently broken contact forms. — [Kickstart Creatives](https://kickstartcreatives.com/blog/squarespace-vs-wix-vs-wordpress) (2026-03-07)
- Clutch: 81% of small businesses have redesigned their website at least once; reasons: modernise design, improve mobile, speed, reorganise content for clarity and conversion. — [Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025)

**Hidden costs, fees, price increases, lock-in (global/US)**
- Wix's own help centre states its SaaS architecture does not support hosting a Wix site elsewhere; blog export is capped at 1,000 items and products at 5,000; limited export of content only. — [hosting.com, quoting Wix help centre](https://hosting.com/blog/why-easy-websites-become-expensive-over-time) (2026-02-16)
- Squarespace exports are partial (store/album/cover/portfolio pages not exportable; 7.1 has no XML export); cancelling takes the site offline within days (agency blog claim). — [hosting.com](https://hosting.com/blog/why-easy-websites-become-expensive-over-time); [Pryce Digital](https://prycedigital.com/blog/hidden-cost-of-website-platform-lock-in) (2026-03-25)
- Reported July 2026 Squarespace price rise (annual billing): Basic $16 to $19, Core $23 to $29, Plus $39 to $49; many customers learned from renewal e-mail; 2% store fee on Basic. — [Webnewbiz](https://webnewbiz.com/squarespace-alternatives) (2026-10-13) (single secondary source)
- Shopify: 0.2-2% extra fee when not using Shopify Payments; Plus around $2,300/month; native B2B/checkout customisation sits on Plus; mid-market merchants routinely spend $350-1,400/month on apps. — [eCommerce Manager](https://ecommercemanager.co/shopify/shopify-alternatives-in-a-cost-first-guide-for-dtc-operators) (2026-07-03) (practitioner source)
- Wix billing/renewal complaints and "4% eCommerce fee ~7% after processing" reports are based on Trustpilot/Reddit anecdotes aggregated by a comparison site. — [CheckThat.ai](https://checkthat.ai/brands/wix/alternatives) (2026-06-25) (anecdotal)
- Migration economics (secondary): migration quotes $2,000-$15,000 for outgrown platforms; research on 892 domain migrations found average 523 days to recover search traffic and 17% never recovered; agency blogs estimate $7,000-$20,000 to leave Wix. — [hosting.com](https://hosting.com/blog/why-easy-websites-become-expensive-over-time); [Pryce Digital](https://prycedigital.com/blog/hidden-cost-of-website-platform-lock-in) (both vendors/agencies with an interest)
- Cost-driven migration flows: 31,946 stores moved to Shopify from seven competitors vs 10,271 moved away (3.1:1); 62.8% of departures from Shopify went to WooCommerce, 23.0% to Wix; reasons are recurring fees, app-stack creep, and closed-ecosystem limits. — [TechnologyChecker](https://technologychecker.io/blog/shopify-migration-insights-analytics) (crawl-based vendor data)

**WordPress maintenance, plugin updates, hacked sites (global)**
- 11,334 new WordPress-ecosystem vulnerabilities found in 2025 (+42% vs 2024); 91% in plugins, 9% in themes, 6 in core; 46% had no developer fix at public disclosure; median time to first exploit for heavily targeted flaws ~5 hours, about half of high-impact flaws exploited within 24 hours; premium/freemium components had 3x the known-exploited vulnerabilities of free ones; common host/network defences (incl. Cloudflare) blocked 12% (WordPress-specific) to 26% (generic) of attacks in Patchstack tests. — [Patchstack, State of WordPress Security 2026](https://patchstack.com/whitepaper/state-of-wordpress-security-in-2026) (2026-02-12); [secondary summary](https://pulse.adyog.com/insights/wordpress-patching-not-enough-patchstack) (2026-10-28). (Patchstack sells WordPress protection; treat as vendor research.)
- 2026 examples: Elementor Pro file-upload RCE (CVE-2026-32475, attacked the same day as the fix); All-in-One WP Migration SQLi (~65% of 5M+ sites still unpatched ~2 weeks after fix). — [DataYard](https://datayard.us/cybersecurity/wordpress-plugin-vulnerabilities-2026) (2026-10-15) (secondary)
- Hosting providers themselves report 41% of 446 surveyed providers losing customers to SaaS (Wix, Shopify) because customers "don't want to manage any aspect"; price sensitivity is still the top churn cause (56%). CloudLinux/WebPros 2026 Web Hosting Trends survey. — [HostingAdvice](https://hostingadvice.com/blog/41-of-web-hosts-are-now-losing-customers-to-wix-and-shopify) (2026-05-20)

**Poor support**
- Support-quality complaints about Wix, GoDaddy etc. are anecdotal (Trustpilot/Reddit quotes); no quantified survey found. Reddit r/WebsiteSEO thread (Jan 2026): users call GoDaddy's builder "slow" and "weakest" for SEO; SEO freelancers say they refuse to work on Wix sites. — [Reddit r/WebsiteSEO](https://reddit.com/r/WebsiteSEO/comments/1q5pc3t/wix_vs_squarespace_vs_godaddy_website_builder_for) (2026-01-06) (anecdotal, contradicted by other commenters who report strong Wix SEO)

**Agency dependency and expensive change requests (UK/IE/US, anecdotal/practitioner)**
- UK agency-client report (Raccoon Web, 2026, collecting founder/marketer testimonials): "hidden charges never stopped" (nearly double the quote by launch), "£3k a month, contract was five PowerPoint slides", "our site is so locked down we have to build a new one", "every single change has to go through the agency; it takes weeks". — [Raccoon Web](https://raccoonweb.co.uk/the-state-of-uk-web-agencies-report) (2026-05-20) (an agency's lead-gen research; not a random survey)
- Real quotes cited: £250 to update a footer icon, £400-600 for minor content edits, £1,500 to remove a single tab. — [Raccoon Web case](https://raccoonweb.co.uk/250-for-a-footer-change-1500-to-remove-a-tab) (2026-01-21)
- Typical hourly agency change rates in Ireland €75-150 with minimums. — [Web60](https://web60.ie/blog/control-your-own-website) (2026-05-31). Maintenance plans ~$250-700/month; "legit retainer" $1,500+/month. — [Classic City](https://classiccity.com/website-changes-need-a-developer) (2026-10-08)
- 87% of 150 marketers wanted more control over web content without a developer (Netlify 2024 leadership report; vendor with a product to sell). — [Construct Digital citing Netlify](https://constructdigital.com/blog/website-updates-need-a-developer) (2026-09-22)
- Diagnostic framing from practitioners: slow small changes come from queue position, process hand-offs, or sites built so only a developer can edit. — [By Alkali](https://byalkali.com/signs/website-changes-take-weeks) (2026-08-13)

**Norway-specific compliance pain (webshops/websites)**
- Private-sector sites and webshops aimed at Norwegian customers must meet 35 WCAG 2.0 A/AA criteria regardless of company size (Likestillings- og diskrimineringsloven §18 + IKT-forskriften); Uutilsynet can order fixes (typically 8 weeks) and impose daily fines (tvangsmulkt). Reported Uutilsynet figures: 98% of sites/apps checked since 2015 broke the minimum requirements; of 90 sites with breaches, 68 fixed, 22 got tvangsmulkt decisions. EU Accessibility Act applies in the EU from 28 June 2025 but, per these sources, had not been incorporated into Norwegian law. — [Fokus Digital](https://fokusdigital.no/artikler/universell-utforming-nettside) (2026-07-08); [Nettsmed](https://nettsmed.no/universell-utforming-nettside) (2026-10-02); [Webtoget](https://webtoget.no/artikler/universell-utforming-nettside) (2026-09-25). These are agency blogs citing Uutilsynet; verify against uutilsynet.no/lovdata before any customer-facing claim (see regelverksradar rule).

### Inferences
- The "unfinished site" and "site that nobody visits" problems are mainly about guidance and content (what to write, what to do next), not tools: All About Cookies shows design/layout, security and custom functionality as blockers; practitioner sources add "blank page, don't know the headline". An AI first draft + a clear checklist to "live and findable" addresses the blocker directly.
- WordPress/plugin maintenance is a structural risk for non-technical owners (91% of vulns in plugins, 5-hour exploitation window, 46% unpatched at disclosure). A managed, plugin-light or non-plugin architecture is a credible selling point against WordPress, and hosting-provider data (41% losing customers to SaaS to avoid managing infrastructure) supports it.
- The key complaint against closed builders is exit cost and fee creep; the key complaint against agencies is permission/cost per small change. A platform offering full export + owner-editable content + flat pricing speaks to both.
- Universal design (UU) is a Norway-specific legal pain point that most DIY builders and many agencies do not guarantee; it can be a differentiator (compliant-by-default templates, accessibility checks) if the legal facts are confirmed.

### Gaps
- No quantified Norwegian pain-point survey (Fiken, DNB, Innovasjon Norge, NHO, Virke, Visma, SpareBank 1, Digital Norway) found for website/webshop ownership.
- Support-quality comparisons across builders are anecdotal; no CSAT/NPS dataset found.
- Hacked-site prevalence among small business websites (share of sites compromised per year) not found; only vulnerability counts.
- Agency pricing/time-to-change data is practitioner testimony; the "Clutch 2024 report" numbers quoted by tuesday.is (7-10 days for a change, $1,200/yr retainer etc.) could not be traced to a Clutch primary source and were excluded.

## 3. Why website-builder customers churn (esp. first 90 days) and what measurably improves retention

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced churn driver is non-completion: roughly half of people who start a DIY site abandon it (US survey), and hosting-industry commentary says template builders see only ~15-25% completion vs 55-80% for AI-first builders (vendor claim). Time-to-first-publish has fallen sharply (Hostinger 6.6 days to 1.9 days to 0.8 days), and activation/onboarding shows up everywhere as the lever. Hard cohort churn benchmarks for builders in the first 90 days were not found in reliable sources.

### Cited Findings
- 51% of people who start building a website abandon it before finishing; 22% never return; 29% finish later with outside help; only about 25% finish without significant problems. US adults n=1,000, May 2025. — [All About Cookies](https://allaboutcookies.org/web-builder-statistics)
- Hostinger internal data (published 2026-05-15): traditional drag-and-drop Website Builder time from creation to first publish 1.9 days (down from 6.6 days in Oct 2024); its AI product Horizons 0.8 days (about 2 days in mid-2025); 93-95% of Horizons users are first-time site builders; 51% of builder sites are company/business sites, ~31% ecommerce (18% product sales, 7.4% digital products, 6% appointment-based). — [Hostinger](https://hostinger.com/blog/ai-website-builder) (vendor-reported)
- Vendor claim: hosting providers moving from template builder to AI builder saw new-signup "completion rate" rise from ~20% to 55-75%; template builders typically 15-25%, AI builders 55-80%. — [BrandForge](https://brandforge.com/ai-website-builder-vs-templates) (2026-08-31) (unsourced vendor claim; the cited Baymard link is a cart-abandonment benchmark, not website-builder data)
- Hosting-industry commentary: "failure rate sits at around 95%" of small-business owners who try to finish a website alone; unfinished sites drift away within about 11 months and get logged as price sensitivity. — [webhosting.today](https://webhosting.today/2026/09/15/why-unfinished-websites-are-costing-hosting-companies-more-than-they-think) (2026-09-15) (opinion piece, no methodology)
- GoDaddy (investor statements via trade press): its AI-driven onboarding (Airo, bundled with every new domain) cohorts attach a second product 30% faster than non-Airo customers; ~10% of its base spends >$500/year. — [webhosting.today](https://webhosting.today/2026/06/29/ai-website-builders-are-now-part-of-provisioning-a-godaddy-outage-just-showed-why) (2026-06-29)
- Aggregated benchmark site (methodology not shown): website builders average 5.2% monthly / 48.3% annual churn in 2026 (SaaS median 4.8% / 43%; top quartile 2.0% / 22%); top cancellation reasons "website project is complete" 32%, "business closes or pauses" 24%; median ARPU $16. — [RetentionCheck](https://retentioncheck.com/churn-benchmarks/website-builders) (2026-05-06) (low confidence)
- Agency-side retention (secondary blog quoting 2026 reports not independently verified): 43% of B2B client churn occurs in the first 90 days (Moxo 2026); project-based agencies ~42% annual client churn (avg lifespan 24 months) vs retainer agencies ~18% (56 months) (Focus Digital 2026); clients completing intake within 48 hours retain >85% vs <50% when it takes 10+ days (OnboardMap); poor onboarding drives 23% of churn (Customer Success Association). Post-launch failure patterns listed: speed decay, plugin conflicts, broken forms/integrations, security incidents, SEO reality gap (no traffic spike), content stagnation. — [XceedBD](https://xceedbd.com/blog/software-development/post-launch-client-retention-agencies) (2026-07-22)
- Hosting providers' own survey (CloudLinux/WebPros, 446 providers, 2026): price sensitivity 56% is the primary churn cause; 41% say customers leave for SaaS builders. — [HostingAdvice](https://hostingadvice.com/blog/41-of-web-hosts-are-now-losing-customers-to-wix-and-shopify)
- A practitioner 90-day checklist: weekly analytics checks, regular content, Search Console/GA setup, collect testimonials, email list, small ad test. — [Excalibur Interactive](https://excaliburinteractive.io/blogs/why-most-business-websites-fail-in-the-first-90-days-and-how-to-avoid-it) (2025-10-25) (opinion)
- "Neglect" pattern after launch: owners stop updating because no payoff or fear of breaking things, not lack of will. — [Kickstart Creatives](https://kickstartcreatives.com/blog/squarespace-vs-wix-vs-wordpress)

### Inferences
- Activation (publishing a real, findable site with the business's real content) is the leading retention lever; every independent source points to time-to-first-publish and "what do I write" as the early-churn moments. Concrete levers supported by the data: AI-generated first draft from business facts (Brønnøysund/org.nr, Google Business Profile import), same-day publish, guided "first 30 days" checklist (GBP, reviews, Search Console), and visible early results (leads, calls, visits).
- The second churn wave is "project done / no payoff": cancellations because the site "is complete" (32% in the weak benchmark) suggest recurring value must be shown after launch (lead inbox, review requests, AI-visibility report, content prompts), otherwise a cheap subscription is cancelled once the owner thinks the job is done.
- Agency churn evidence (first-90-day cliff, intake speed, retainers) is mostly secondary and unverified, but directionally consistent with the builder side: relationship and measurable value in the first 90 days decide retention.

### Gaps
- No primary, reliable cohort data on 30/60/90-day retention or publish rate for Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy, Shopify or Norwegian builders (Mystore, 24Nettbutikk). The RetentionCheck, BrandForge and webhosting.today figures are low-confidence.
- No controlled evidence on which interventions "measurably" improve retention beyond vendor-reported time-to-publish and activation claims.
- No Norwegian churn data at all.

## 4. Agency / freelancer vs DIY builder: why people choose each, and what they distrust about cheap builders

### Takeaway
In the best US survey, small businesses split roughly 45% agency / 37% in-house team / 9% owner alone / 9% freelancer / 1% friend or family, so DIY-by-owner is the minority outcome and freelancers a small slice (US). People pick professionals for custom features, strategy, SEO/conversion and "being taken seriously"; they pick DIY for budget, control and speed. Distrust of builders centres on generic look, SEO ceiling, performance (increasingly outdated), accessibility, and lock-in.

### Cited Findings
- Who built small-business sites (US, n=406, Aug 2025): outsourced to agency 45%; built in-house 37%; built by the owner 9%; outsourced to freelancer/consultant 9%; friend/family 1%. 54% hire a professional vs 46% build themselves (Clutch's rollup). — [Clutch SBW 2025](https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025); [Clutch DIY vs agency](https://clutch.co/resources/diy-website-versus-agency-web-design-outsourcing) (2025-09-10)
- Platform mix of small-business sites: 41% no-code builders (Wix, Squarespace), 34% low-code (WordPress, Shopify), 12% custom code. — [Clutch SBW 2025](https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025)
- Clutch's own framing: DIY platforms $20-50/month excluding hosting/plug-ins; agencies by the hour $25-149 (most Clutch-reviewed web projects under $10,000; US agencies typically $100-149/hr); DIY "often fine for local and in-person businesses", agency "expected for ecommerce and SaaS"; custom checkout, AI chatbots, complex features favour agencies. — [Clutch DIY vs agency](https://clutch.co/resources/diy-website-versus-agency-web-design-outsourcing); [Clutch pricing](https://clutch.co/web-designers/pricing)
- Who buys web development on Clutch (23,346 verified reviews, Jan-Oct 2026): 51% of web development reviews come from companies with 1-10 employees; owners/founders/partners 31% of reviewers, C-suite 24%; 52% in executive leadership. — [Clutch, Who's Actually Buying B2B Services](https://clutch.co/resources/who-buys-b2b-services) (2026-10-15)
- What buyers look for in an agency partner (Clutch editorial): portfolio, client reviews, technical expertise, curiosity about the client's business, communication. Quality of work (27%) before cost (23%). — [Clutch](https://clutch.co/topdesignfirms/web-design/blog/top-schools-for-web-design); [Clutch tech innovation](https://clutch.co/resources/invest-tech-meet-customer-expectations)
- Distrust / limits of cheap builders (practitioner views): template ceiling and "generic look"; Squarespace conversion structure and customisation walls; Wix "design drift"; SEO control limited on closed platforms; builder sites "can't be fully ADA/accessibility compliant". — [Treble Tree](https://trebletree.co/insights/signs-youve-outgrown-your-diy-website) (2026-10-19); [YouGrow.pro](https://yougrow.pro/blog/why-diy-website-costing-you-customers) (2025-11-11) (accessibility claim is advocacy-sourced, not tested here)
- Counter-evidence: the "builders are slow / bad for SEO" belief is partly outdated (HTTP Archive CWV: Wix ~75%, Squarespace ~70% vs WordPress 46%); an agency author concedes builders are enough for disposable/campaign pages, and that builder pricing "teaser" rates renew at 3-4x with a realistic $17-29/month for a plan that supports a custom domain without branding. — [Francis Web Agency](https://franciswebagency.com/insights/ai-website-builder-vs-custom-web-design) (2026-08-01)
- Reddit (r/WebsiteSEO, Jan 2026): SEO practitioners say they "won't touch" Wix sites and that GoDaddy's builder is weakest; others report Wix/Squarespace rank fine for local businesses if set up properly; consensus: content quality, links, speed and who maintains it matter more than platform. — [Reddit thread](https://reddit.com/r/WebsiteSEO/comments/1q5pc3t/wix_vs_squarespace_vs_godaddy_website_builder_for)
- Why businesses leave agencies: slow, expensive small edits; sites "locked down" so only the agency can change them; unclear contracts; hourly vs retainer mismatch. Remedies cited: block/page libraries owners can safely edit, two-lane arrangement (client owns routine content; agency owns structural work), written turnaround classes. — [Classic City](https://classiccity.com/website-changes-need-a-developer); [By Alkali](https://byalkali.com/signs/website-changes-take-weeks); [Construct Digital](https://constructdigital.com/blog/website-updates-need-a-developer)
- Norwegian price anchors for the professional route (vendor self-published): nettside from 16,900 kr (Hellpass); agency nettside 25,000-150,000 kr (Visionmedia); agency-built WooCommerce 40,000-100,000 kr plus hosting 500-1,500 kr/month; SaaS webshop 400-2,000 kr/month; Smartbyrå managed webshop 799 kr/month with no binding; SEO retainers 8,000-25,000 kr/month for small/mid businesses. — [Hellpass](https://hellpass.no/hva-koster-digital-markedsforing-i-norge) (2026-05-07); [Visionmedia](https://visionmedia.no/blog/markedsforingsbyraa-guide) (2026-01-27); [Infoframe](https://infoframe.no/artikler/lage-nettbutikk); [Smartbyrå](https://smartbyra.no/nettbutikk/vipps-klarna-og-stripe-betalingslosninger) (2026-04-05); [Byråmatch](https://xn--byrmatch-c0a.no/fagbloggen/hva-koster-digital-markedsfoering-for-smaa-bedrifter) (2026-05-06)

### Inferences
- A "managed" hybrid fits the evidenced gap between DIY (owner abandons or neglects) and agency (owner dependent, slow/expensive changes): the owner edits routine content safely; the provider handles hosting, security, updates and structural work for a flat fee.
- "Generic look" and "not serious" are real credibility concerns, but the 2025-2026 evidence is that performance and basic SEO are no longer the strongest objections to builders; lock-in/exit cost, SEO-ceiling perception among professionals, and accessibility are stronger. Position on portability (full export, own domain/DNS, no hostage content) and on proof (CWV scores, schema, UU checks).
- Freelancers are a small share (9%) of US small-business builds, but Clutch's data counts "in-house team" (37%) separately, so the true "person who builds for me" share may be larger; also Clutch reviews skew to agencies. For Norway the freelancer-or-bureau choice is likely more relevant than US data suggests but is unmeasured.

### Gaps
- No Norwegian survey on how businesses choose between DIY builder, bureau (byrå) and freelancer; no Norwegian data on share of sites built by each route.
- No quantified survey of "what small-business owners distrust about cheap builders" (generic look, "not serious", SEO doubts, lock-in); evidence is practitioner opinion.
- No survey on how often agency clients feel "locked in" (code/hosting/domain ownership); only testimonials.

## 5. Jobs-to-be-done and post-launch wants (calls/leads, bookings, selling, credibility; reporting, reviews, GBP, social, newsletter, simple updates)

### Takeaway
The jobs are, in order of evidence: get found and be believed (credibility), turn visitors into contacts/calls/bookings, and for sellers, take payment smoothly (Vipps in Norway). After launch the evidenced needs are fresh/accurate info (hours, prices, services), reviews (volume, recency, replies), Google Business Profile upkeep, and now AI-answer visibility. Owners also want to avoid developer dependency for small edits.

### Cited Findings
- Credibility/sales motives: increase sales 39%, build brand awareness/credibility 24%, provide information/customer support 22% (Clutch, US 2025). For non-selling owners, 59-73% (UK to US) say their website is critical for marketing and credibility; 42-61% rely on it for customer communications (GoDaddy, 2025). — [Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025); [GoDaddy](https://godaddy.com/research/explore-the-data/annual-report-2025)
- Businesses with websites report SEO as top lead source (40%), social top for 10% (Clutch US 2025). Google/Deloitte (cited secondhand): companies with a website were 2.8x more likely to report revenue growth (4,500+ small businesses; date/original not verified). — [Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025); [Concat](https://concat.pro/page/en-US/blog/small-business-websites-roi-data) (2026-07-30)
- SMB lead channels (BrightLocal SMB report 2026, US, n>1,000): word-of-mouth 35%, social media 33%, AI tools 23%, direct website traffic 22%, Google 22% (top-two channel rankings by owners), despite 71% of consumers using Google in local search. — [BrightLocal via PR Newswire](http://prnewswire.com/news-releases/brightlocal-report-small-businesses-losing-online-visibility-as-social-and-ai-prioritised-over-google-302916401.html)
- After reading positive reviews, 54% of consumers visit the business website; 66% do more research (34% ready to buy/book). — [BrightLocal LCRS 2026 via Sidekick](https://sidekickdigital.com/blog/what-brightlocals-2026-local-consumer-review-survey-means-for-your-business)
- Reviews: 78% of consumers were asked for a review in the last 12 months; of those asked, 83% wrote one per Sidekick Digital's summary but 65% per BrightLocal's own page (the two sources conflict; either way asking works); 28% "always" write one if asked; 94% open to writing reviews; 89% expect owner replies. 11% of consumers were offered an incentive for a positive review, which BrightLocal notes could bring a fine under the US FTC online-reviews rule. — [Sidekick Digital](https://sidekickdigital.com/blog/what-brightlocals-2026-local-consumer-review-survey-means-for-your-business); [BrightLocal LCRS 2026](https://brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review)
- Review ecosystem is fragmenting (US, 2026): consumers use six review sites on average; Google 71% (83% in 2025), Facebook 49%, Yelp 44%, YouTube 43%, Apple Maps 27%, ChatGPT 45%. — [ReviewMankey summarising BrightLocal](https://reviewmankey.com/blog/online-review-statistics-2026)
- Contact info and opening hours are important to 85% of consumers; "clear pricing or an offer" is a top-3 reason for choosing a local business (29%). — [BrightLocal](https://brightlocal.com/research/consumer-search-behavior); [Timpson](https://timpsonmarketing.com/blog/do-customers-use-chatgpt-to-find-local-businesses)
- What SMBs want AI to know about them: specific products/services 58%, who they are 54%, brand awareness vs competitors 49%; operational details (live availability, discounts, shipping windows) ranked lower. — [Squarespace, New Front Door](https://fr.squarespace.com/blog/ai-search-for-business-owners)
- Content updates: 61% of small businesses update weekly; owners avoid editing out of fear of breaking layouts (practitioner). — [Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025); [Kickstart](https://kickstartcreatives.com/blog/squarespace-vs-wix-vs-wordpress)
- Social vs website: GoDaddy 2025: 77% of US microbusiness owners say social is important for awareness, but only 24% sell directly on social; UK: 32% say social is primary for attracting customers. — [GoDaddy](https://godaddy.com/research/explore-the-data/annual-report-2025)
- Newsletter/email: no survey data located on small-business newsletter wants; ContentMonk/Klaviyo tools exist but no stat sourced.
- Norwegian webshop jobs: checkout with Vipps (+ card, Klarna for AOV above ~500-1,500 kr), shipping integrations (Bring/Posten, PostNord, Helthjem, nShift), accounting sync (Fiken, Tripletex, PowerOffice Go, Visma eAccounting). — [Infoframe](https://infoframe.no/artikler/lage-nettbutikk); [Nettpilot](https://nettpilot.no/hvilken-betalingslosning-bor-du-velge-til-din-nettbutikk); [Webkonsepter](https://webkonsepter.no/starte-nettbutikk) (practitioner/vendor guides)
- Fiken is described as the default accounting tool for Norwegian sole proprietors/freelancers from ~149 kr/month; accounting integration is a recurring selection criterion for Norwegian webshops. — [Smartbyrå](https://smartbyra.no/regnskap-og-okonomi/beste-regnskapsprogram-for-enkeltpersonforetak) (2026-04-05); [Smartok](https://smartok.no/blogg/beste-regnskapsprogram) (2026-04-03) (comparison sites)
- Norwegian entrepreneurs' population (size): 656,492 establishments in 2026, of which 448,658 (68.3%) have no employees, 103,298 (15.7%) have 1-4, 40,367 (6.1%) have 5-9, 30,803 (4.7%) 10-19, 22,336 (3.4%) 20-49, 6,962 (1.1%) 50-99, 3,081 (0.5%) 100-249, 987 (0.2%) 250+. — [SSB, Bedrifter](https://ssb.no/en/virksomheter-foretak-og-regnskap/virksomheter-og-foretak/statistikk/virksomheter) (2026-01-12). Note: establishments, not legal entities.

### Inferences
- The strongest "job" for the micro/solo segment is not "have a website" but "be chosen": complete info, reviews, clear price/offer, tap-to-call, correct hours. A platform that bundles website + GBP sync + review requests + a lead inbox covers the jobs consumers actually use to decide.
- Review volume and recency thresholds (20+ reviews, last 3 months) and AI-citation behaviour make review collection a platform feature, not an add-on. Beware: in Norway an incentive for reviews is a legal and ethical risk (Helene's own rule), so prompts should ask for feedback, not pay for positives.
- AI visibility reporting is a new "reporting" job: owners cannot measure it today (56% cite ROI measurement as top challenge; Squarespace launched an "AI Visibility" tool in July 2026), so a simple "what do ChatGPT/Google say about my business" check is a sellable feature.
- Over two thirds of Norwegian establishments are sole operators (no employees), so the ENK/solo segment is the largest by count; they typically need a one-page/simple site, booking/contact, Vipps links, and zero upkeep.

### Gaps
- No Norwegian survey of jobs-to-be-done for small business websites.
- No sourced data on demand for newsletter, booking, or social-feed integration among small businesses.
- Share of Norwegian solo businesses that currently have a website not found for micro (<10 employees); Eurostat only covers 10+ (see Q7).

## 6. Larger companies (mid-market / enterprise): integrations, multi-language, B2B pricing, roles, SLA, security, headless

### Takeaway
Mid-market and enterprise buyers decide on integration depth (ERP/PIM/CRM/procurement), B2B price logic (customer-specific price lists, contract pricing, reordering), and architecture flexibility; "composable/headless" is mainstream among very large firms but adoption is mostly partial, expensive and skills-heavy. Sound hard data on SLAs, roles/permissions and compliance purchasing criteria is scarce.

### Cited Findings
- B2B buyer priorities (900 C-suite/procurement, Netconomy, Oct 2025): price optimisation/transparency, automated reordering 56%, procurement-system integration 43%, real-time inventory visibility 42%; 83% say strong self-service is crucial in vendor choice; 43% still want a sales rep for complex purchases. — [Netconomy](https://netconomy.net/blog/b2b-self-service-features)
- Eurostat 2025 (EU, enterprises 10+ employees): 79.0% have a website (large 95.7%, medium 89.7%, small 76.7%); most common website functions: description of goods/services and price lists 66.4%, job ads 31.3%, content in at least 2 languages 29.5%; only 22.5% take orders via the site; e-sales 2024: 21.4% of small, 31.7% of medium, 48.5% of large enterprises, with EDI-type sales 17.5% of small e-sellers vs 38.3% of medium e-sellers (EDI turnover exceeds web turnover overall: 11.07% vs 8.39% of total turnover). — [Eurostat: Digital economy and society - enterprises](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained?title=Digital_economy_and_society_statistics_-_enterprises) (2026-06-02); [Eurostat e-sales](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/SEPDF/cache/14386.pdf) (2026-02-27); [Knowledge Engineers summary](https://theknowledgeengineers.com/reports/digital-marketing-europe) (2026-09-04)
- MACH Alliance 2025 Global Annual Research (561 IT decision-makers, firms with 5,000+ employees and USD 500M+ revenue; US/UK/DE/FR/CA/AU): 87% say MACH widely implemented; 9 in 10 say it met or exceeded ROI; 93% increased MACH infrastructure in the past year; expected 61% of stack MACH/composable by 2026; barriers: investment trade-off unclear 31%, internal resistance 29%, no budget 22%, skills and lock-in concerns ~20-26%; benefits: customer experience 55%, systems integration 54%, agility 50%, reduced IT cost 42%. Skills required are higher than for monoliths. — [Webtonic summary](https://webtonic.io/blog/headless-commerce-statistics) (2026-09-21); [MNT Future summary](https://mntfuture.com/blog/composable-commerce-marketplace-migration) (2026-08-12) (MACH Alliance is a vendor trade body)
- Gartner figures cited secondhand (not verified at source): 71% of enterprise B2B orgs adopted at least one composable principle but only 18% fully composable; ~85% of DXP program effort goes to integrations; 70% of organisations "mandated" to adopt composable DXP by end 2026. — [Bemeir](https://bemeir.com/articles/adobe-commerce-b2b-integrations-custom-workflows-trend-analysis-2) (2026-04-17); [Practical Logix](https://practicallogix.com/the-composable-dxp-mandate-of-2026-why-70-of-enterprises-will-be-forced-off-their-monolithic-dxp-and-which-ones-are-actually-ready) (2026-06-03) (both consultancies)
- Traditional vs headless (vendor comparison): headless takes 4-9 months vs 4-12 weeks for themed builds; B2B account hierarchy and contract pricing "plugin-dependent, often brittle" on traditional platforms, modelled natively in headless; headless adds engineering overhead. Practitioner thresholds: under $1M revenue headless rarely makes sense; above ~$25M it wins on 3-year TCO. — [Elastic Path](https://elasticpath.com/blog/headless-commerce-vs-traditional-commerce) (2026-08-07); [Refact](https://refact.co/insights/ecommerce/headless-ecommerce-cms) (2026-06-24) (vendor/consultancy)
- B2B pain points on monoliths: bolted-on contract pricing, volume tiers, customer-specific catalogues, multi-currency; channel proliferation; release velocity; brittle ERP/PIM/WMS integrations; vendor lock-in. — [McKenna Consultants](https://mckennaconsultants.com/mach-architecture-migration-planning-your-move-from-monolithic-commerce) (2025-08-12)
- Platform tier gaps: Shopify B2B and deep checkout customisation require Plus (~$2,300/month; UK source "£2,000/month minimum"); BigCommerce includes customer-specific prices and price-list management natively whereas Shopify typically needs apps (Norwegian comparison). — [eCommerce Manager](https://ecommercemanager.co/shopify/shopify-alternatives-in-a-cost-first-guide-for-dtc-operators); [Innatus](https://innatus.digital/digital-insights/the-hidden-costs-of-platform-lock-in) (2025-04-11); [Nettbutikksguiden](https://nettbutikksguiden.no/bigcommerce) (2025-12-04)
- ERP integration is "the most crucial part" for manufacturers; MVP with light ERP integration "low to mid seven figures" USD, complex multi-system rollouts high seven figures to over $2M (Shopify's own B2B guide, vendor). — [Shopify Norge, B2B for produsenter](https://shopify.com/no/blog/b2b-e-handel-for-produsenter) (2026-03-27)
- Norway IT-supplier buyer survey (Radar Norway, Jan 2026): buyers want proactivity, long-term thinking and continuous improvement; suppliers over-focus on incident handling and SLA compliance. — [Radar Group](https://radargrp.com/no/om-oss/press/radar-karer-norges-ledende-it-leverandorer-i-2025-her-er-selskapene-som-topper-kundenes-rangering) (2026-01-28)
- Compliance for Norwegian enterprises with public-facing sites: UU requirement (see Q2); GDPR/data residency and EU hosting are repeatedly cited in Norwegian buyer content, but no survey percentages were found.
- Norwegian AI adoption by firm size (proxy for digital maturity): SSB 2026: nearly 5 in 10 firms with 10+ employees use AI (3 in 10 in 2025, 1 in 10 in 2023); about 4 in 10 of firms with 10-19 employees; about 8 in 10 of firms with 100+ employees. — [SSB, Bruken av KI i næringslivet akselererer](https://ssb.no/teknologi-og-innovasjon/informasjons-og-kommunikasjonsteknologi-ikt/statistikk/bruk-av-ikt-i-naeringslivet/artikler/bruken-av-ki-i-naeringslivet-akselererer) (2026-09-25). 2025: 77% of non-users said they lack competence, about 4 in 10 worry about data protection/legal clarity. — [SSB 2025](https://ssb.no/teknologi-og-innovasjon/informasjons-og-kommunikasjonsteknologi-ikt/statistikk/bruk-av-ikt-i-naeringslivet/artikler/bruken-av-ki-har-skutt-fart-det-siste-aret) (2025-09-24)

### Inferences
- A Norwegian platform's credible mid-market path is likely a modular/API-first core with first-class connectors for the systems Norwegian firms actually run (Fiken only at the bottom; Tripletex, PowerOffice Go, Visma, Xledger and ERP at the top) rather than a pure SaaS template product; ERP/PIM integration and customer-specific pricing are the make-or-break features, and headless is not warranted below roughly the 1M USD revenue band (practitioner heuristic, unverified).
- SLA/security evidence is thin; "proactivity vs incident handling" (Radar) suggests buyers evaluate a vendor by how it prevents problems, which is consistent with managed security/updates as a selling point.
- Multi-language: only 29.5% of EU enterprise websites offer 2+ languages, so it is a differentiating but minority requirement; for Norwegian exporters it is expected to be higher but is unmeasured.

### Gaps
- No survey data on uptime SLA expectations, roles/permissions/SSO requirements, security certifications (ISO 27001, SOC 2), data residency, or procurement criteria specific to Norwegian mid-market buyers.
- Gartner/Forrester figures are secondhand through consultancies; primary reports not accessed.
- No Norwegian B2B ecommerce survey (customer-specific prices, ERP integration) found; the Inbound/MFO B2B report (650 Norwegian B2B firms, Oct-Dec 2024, [PDF](https://inbound.no/hubfs/B2B_report_2025_Inbound.pdf)) exists but no website/ecommerce figures were extracted.
- The NIS2/digitalsikkerhetsloven effect on website/platform procurement in Norway was not researched.

## 7. Norwegian-specific data (Fiken, DNB, Innovasjon Norge, NHO, Virke, Digital Norway, Visma, SpareBank 1, SSB)

### Takeaway
Direct Norwegian surveys on website/webshop needs from the named bodies were not found. What exists is: SSB/NHO data on AI adoption, Eurostat website penetration for firms with 10+ employees, SSB establishment counts, Virke/PostNord e-commerce growth, and commercial price/market anecdotes. Norway lags Denmark and Sweden on small-firm website penetration.

### Cited Findings
- Eurostat 2025, Norway: 85.4% of firms with 10-49 employees have a website (Denmark 94%, Sweden 90%); 87.0% of firms with 10+ employees; micro firms (<10) not counted by Eurostat. About 1 in 7 Norwegian small firms have no website. ### Inferences
- Norway's small-firm website penetration gap vs Denmark/Sweden (85% vs 94%/90% for 10-49 employees) and the high share of sole-operator establishments (68%) imply a large untapped base of micro firms for a simple, cheap, low-effort product, but the micro segment is not measured by Eurostat.
- AI adoption is accelerating in Norwegian business but small firms trail large ones, and non-users cite competence not technology; the same "I don't know how" barrier appears in the Squarespace AI-search data, supporting guided/AI-assisted onboarding as the right abstraction.

### Gaps
- Searched but not found: Fiken, DNB, Innovasjon Norge, Visma, SpareBank 1, Digital Norway, Virke website-needs surveys; NHO "Kompetansebarometer" and Digital Norway KI material concern AI skills, not websites.
- Not accessed: SSB statbank tables for share of Norwegian firms with websites, e-commerce share (SSB table 10974), social media use; Eurostat isoc_ciweb original; Norstat/Bring/Posten "E-handel i Norden" primary reports; Brønnøysund start-up statistics.
- Norwegian-language AI search behaviour (ChatGPT/Perplexity/AI Overviews in Norwegian) not measured in any source found.

## 8. Cross-cutting: source quality and how to use these notes

### Takeaway
The most reliable primary instruments here are Eurostat/SSB/NHO (Norway/EU), and survey-based reports from Clutch, BrightLocal, All About Cookies, GoDaddy and Squarespace (US-centred, vendor-published, n of roughly 400-2,400). Much of the "churn", "agency dependency" and "builder distrust" evidence is practitioner or vendor content, and several widely repeated statistics could not be traced to a primary source.

### Cited Findings
- Clutch SBW: SurveyMonkey, n=406 US owners, one-day survey (5 Aug 2025); BrightLocal LCRS: n=1,002 US adults (SurveyMonkey, Feb 2026), self-reported; one reviewer found mismatches between BrightLocal's 2026 text and chart data in a few places, so use headline figures (6% to 45% AI, 97% read reviews) with some care. — [Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/state-of-small-business-websites-2025); [CyberLab critique](https://cyberlab.team/blog/local-consumer-review-survey-2026) (2026-09-09)
- Squarespace AI survey caveat from the publisher: self-reported, directional, respondents in North America, UK, Australia. — [Squarespace](https://fr.squarespace.com/blog/ai-search-for-business-owners)
- Patchstack sells WordPress protection; MACH Alliance is an industry body promoting MACH; Netconomy, Hostinger, WP Engine, Netlify, Shopify blogs have commercial interests.

### Inferences
- Report writers should present "AI search" and "builder churn" figures as directional and US-based, and avoid claiming Norwegian equivalents. The strongest Norway claims available are: 68% of establishments have no employees (SSB), small-firm website penetration below Denmark/Sweden (Eurostat), AI adoption speed (SSB), legal UU duty (to be verified), and Vipps/BankID expectations in checkout.

### Gaps
- Items deliberately excluded for lack of a traceable source: "Clutch 2024 SMB Web Services Spending Report" quotes in tuesday.is posts; the Digitano "38% of US retailers moving off single platforms by 2027 (Gartner 2025)" claim; the Shopify 2023 price increase narrative from an agency blog; the "NameLink 92% of AI-built sites pass CWV" test.
- Research limit: search tooling returned mostly English, US-centred and vendor-authored content; primary Norwegian survey reports may exist behind PDFs/registration walls (Virke, Innovasjon Norge, Digital Norway, SpareBank 1 SMB-barometer, DNB SMB-undersøkelsen) and were not located.
