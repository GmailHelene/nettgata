# Bransjebehov: one core platform, many vertical packs (Norway, as of 31 Oct 2026)

Method note: research done 31 Oct 2026 with Keenable search (snippets; few full-page fetches). SSB numbers were pulled directly from the SSB open API (table 07091, year 2026 = status 1 Jan 2026) and the percentages marked "derived" are my own arithmetic on those numbers. Many vendor/comparison sources are commercial and are flagged where bias matters. Prices are ex VAT unless the source says otherwise.

## 1. Which verticals are largest in Norway (by number of businesses), and where are willingness to pay and underserved needs?

### Takeaway
Norway has 656,492 businesses (bedrifter) on 1 Jan 2026, 68% without employees. By NACE division the biggest SMB-relevant verticals are specialist construction/building (43+41, ~66,600), retail (47, 38,493), health services (86, 42,823), arts/entertainment (90, 27,011, but 94% solo), education (85), consulting/technical services (70/71/74/62), personal services incl. hairdressers (96, 18,782), wholesale (46, 19,262) and restaurants (56, 15,215). The number of businesses WITH staff (where multi-user, staff calendar, multi-location needs appear) is the better proxy for willingness to pay: retail ~23,400, health ~11,600, wholesale ~10,100, restaurants ~9,300, construction (43+41) ~24,000, personal services ~5,000.

### Inferences
- Counts alone mislead: arts (90) is the 4th-5th biggest division but 94% have no employees, so it is a huge but low-ARPU segment (volume play, needs a very cheap/free tier). Retail (47), construction (41/43), health (86), restaurants (56) and wholesale (46) have large staffed populations and are better targets for paid packs.
- Highest willingness to pay is where the site is tied to revenue-critical workflows already priced per seat/location: salons/clinics/fitness (booking + payments), clubs (membership + payments), B2B wholesalers (custom B2B shops start ~450k NOK, so even a 20k-60k NOK productised pack undercuts agencies). Chains/lokallag structures (clubs with local branches, salon/clinic groups, franchises) are the clearest underserved segment because mainstream builders price per site.
- Tradespeople (43/41, ~15,300 with employees in 43 alone) mostly lack a pack that ties lead capture, credentials (sentral godkjenning) and quoting to their accounting tool; they depend on the Mittanbud marketplace (see section 2).

### Gaps
- No 4-digit NACE counts (96.02 hairdressers/beauty, 86.2 doctors/dentists/physios, 93.13 fitness, 47.65 toy retail, 47.91 online retail) found; table 07091 only has 2-digit divisions. A 4-digit extract from Brønnøysund (open Enhetsregisteret API by NACE code) is needed.
- No reliable count of registered Norwegian toy shops, fitness centres or hair salons from an official source.
- No reliable Norwegian willingness-to-pay survey by vertical; the price points above are vendor list prices only.
- GiveRadar's 133,109 figure is unverified; the official Frivillighetsregisteret count was not retrieved.

## 2. Per-vertical must-have features, Norwegian integrations, and what "looks credible"

### Takeaway
Each vertical is defined by one "system of record" that the site must plug into (salon booking: Timma/Onlinebooq/Fresha; restaurants: DinnerBooking/Resos; clinics: Pasientsky/Helseboka; clubs: Rubic/Spond/Bloc(IdrettenOnline) with NIF integration; craftsmen: Tripletex + SmartCraft/SmartDok + Mittanbud; B2B: ERP/accounting like Visma, PowerOffice, Tripletex), plus vertical trust signals (CE/age info for toys, sentral godkjenning/elvirksomhetsregister for trades, Google reviews). The core platform should therefore be integration-first (embed/API/iframe + Vipps), not rebuild these systems.

### Cited Findings

#### Cross-vertical Norwegian plumbing
- Payment stack for Norwegian webshops: Vipps MobilePay (Vipps på Nett/Checkout 2.99% + 1 kr under 10 MNOK/yr, down to ~1.99% above), Nets Easy 1.80% + 1 kr (Norwegian/EEA cards), Klarna Checkout 2.79% + 3.50 kr; typical recommended combo Vipps + card (Stripe or Nets) + Klarna for higher basket values. Vipps supports recurring/subscription payments. — [Nettpilot 2026-10-09](https://nettpilot.no/hvilken-betalingslosning-bor-du-velge-til-din-nettbutikk), [Shopify Norge merchant guide 2026-02-24](https://shopify.com/no/blog/merchant-tjenester), [Nettsmed 2026-07-21](https://nettsmed.no/betalingslosning-nettbutikk), [Norsk Næring](https://xn--norsknring-i6a.no/betalingslosning-nettbutikk-norge)
- Norwegian webshop law basics: 14 days angrerett, price/identity/return info, MVA registration above 50,000 NOK/yr, supervised by Forbrukertilsynet/Datatilsynet; product-specific rules for food (allergens), electronics (CE, WEEE), cosmetics (CPNP, Norwegian ingredient list) and toys (CE, EN 71). — [Zennet 2026-06-07](https://zennet.no/krav-til-nettbutikk-norge), [Lexolve](https://lexolve.com/ressurser/starte-bedrift/nettbutikk)

#### Toy webshop
- Toys must follow the Toy Safety Regulation (leketøyforskriften, implements the EU toy directive); manufacturer picks harmonised standard EN 71-1 to 71-8 or third-party approval. CE-marking is mandatory; CE must be visible/legible on toy, label or packaging. All marking, safety information and instructions must be in Norwegian; warnings have placement and visibility requirements "ved salg" (at point of sale). Fag/tilsyn is split between DSB and Miljødirektoratet. — [DSB Leketøy (2024-06-26)](https://dsb.no/produkter-og-forbrukertjenester/produkter/leketoy), [Miljødirektoratet merking](https://miljodirektoratet.no/ansvarsomrader/kjemikalier/for-naringsliv/importere-produsere-og-selge-produkter/merking)
- "China Export" is not a valid CE mark and toys carrying it are illegal to sell in EEA/Norway; importers/distributors must verify CE and declaration of conformity. — [Miljødirektoratet](https://miljodirektoratet.no/ansvarsomrader/kjemikalier/for-naringsliv/importere-produsere-og-selge-produkter/merking)
- Toys must carry manufacturer/importer name and address; age and warning labelling per §§10-11 of the regulation, in Norwegian; EU declaration of conformity (§13); example warning for water toys quoted from Annex V; product-group example for toy bear needs EN 71-1/-2/-3 plus phthalate (REACH) testing. — [DSB internkontroll guide (2024-09-26)](https://dsb.no/produkter-og-forbrukertjenester/veiledning/veileder-i-internkontroll-for-importorer-og-produsenter-av-forbrukerprodukter)
- GPSR (General Product Safety Regulation) applies in the EU from 13 Dec 2024 and tightens traceability/documentation, labelling/contact information, accident notification and recall duties for consumer products not otherwise regulated. — [Innovasjon Norge](https://innovasjonnorge.no/artikkel/er-produktene-dine-omfattet-av-nye-eu-regelverk) (note: snippet; EEA adoption status for Norway not confirmed in this source)
- Salon retail example: a Kristiansand salon site with Timma booking, 3 languages and a new product page reported quadrupled hair-care sales (self-reported by freelancer). — [Sømløs](https://somlos.no/en)

Treat both numbers as unverified competitor claims. — [Klippa](https://klippa.no); contradicted by [Skailand](https://skailandconsulting.no/en/bookingsystem-vs-timma)

- Chain example: Cutters (96 salons in Dec 2021); NHO's bransjeforening Norges Frisør og Velværebedrifter is the employer association. — [NHO](https://nho.no/om-nho/artikler/nho-styrker-posisjonen-som-norges-storste-fellesskap-for-bedrifter)

- Credibility markers: Elvirksomhetsregisteret (DSB) registration is legally required for electrical work; DiBK's sentral godkjenning is a voluntary quality scheme (competence, QA routines, taxes paid) and approved firms may download a godkjenningsmerke (badge) and godkjenningsbevis; DiBK offers a free open REST API (SGregister) for sites/systems to check whether a firm has sentral godkjenning. — [Mittanbud](https://mittanbud.no/proff/aktuelt/sertifiseringer-og-godkjenninger), [DiBK SGregister](https://sgregister.dibk.no/?locale=nn), [DiBK brukerportal](https://sgbruker.dibk.no), [Svenn guide](https://svenn.com/blogg/sentral-godkjenning-guide-sjekkliste), [Mittanbud velg håndverker 2025-09-22](https://mittanbud.no/privatperson/startguide/slik-velger-du-riktig-haandverker)

## 4. Multi-location / chain / franchise needs (central brand control with local pages, per-location booking and SEO)

### Takeaway
Multi-location sites need one brand layer + a Location entity with its own page (own address/hours/staff/photos/reviews/FAQ/LocalBusiness schema), one Google Business Profile per physical location linking to that location's page, and per-location booking links. Templated city-swap pages are the main failure mode. Booking vendors often limit multi-location (Reserve with Google single-location only for some; Resos multi-location is a paid add-on), so the website layer must route to the correct location's booking account.

### Cited Findings
- Google requires a distinct verified Business Profile per physical location; one profile cannot represent several addresses; merging suppresses the others. — [Growth Digital (AU) 2026-10-03](https://growthdigital.com.au/insights/multi-location-seo-franchises-australia-guide), [Trebletree 2026-07-09](https://trebletree.co/insights/multi-location-seo-playbook) (agency sources paraphrasing Google guidelines; primary Google doc not fetched)
- A location page that ranks includes: geo+service headline, intro referencing real neighbourhoods/landmarks, branch name/address/phone and embedded map, local staff bios, location-specific photos, real local reviews, local pricing nuances, local FAQ, LocalBusiness schema per page (name, address, geo, hours, link to the location's profile), and a locations sitemap; keep pages on the main domain in subfolders; avoid city-name-swap duplicates. — [Busyless 2026-07-04](https://busyless.space/blog/multiple-location-seo)
- Franchise checklist: GBP website URL points to that location page; GBP name format matches page and schema; location-specific photos; a Google Post within 30 days; review replies within 7 days; governance (master location data sheet, NAP consistency audits, quarterly refresh). — [Elevative 2026-06-09](https://elevativedigital.com/blog/franchise-local-seo), [Optiseon 2026-09-01](https://optiseon.com/blog/multi-location-franchise-seo-2026)
- Franchise model: corporate sets standards, franchisees execute locally; cooperatives (member-owned, shared brand, independently run locations) have the same problem. — [Trebletree](https://trebletree.co/insights/multi-location-seo-playbook)
- Google Business Profile performance can be viewed per profile or aggregated (calls, clicks, directions, searches), but includes organic and ads. — [Search Sherpa 2026-10-08](https://thesearchsherpa.com/multi-location-seo)
- Booking-system limits: Reserve with Google via Salonized requires a single-location business (multi-location not supported); Resos multi-location and API are paid add-ons; DinnerBooking has multi-venue overview and moves guests to another venue when full; NordBooq includes several locations in all plans. — [Salonized help](http://help.salonized.com/en/articles/3924450-what-is-reserve-with-google-and-how-does-it-work), [Resos](https://foodbusinessapps.com/sites/site/resos), [DinnerBooking](https://foodbusinessapps.com/sites/site/dinnerbooking), [NordBooq](https://nordbooq.com/en/blog/bedste-bookingsystem-til-frisoer)
- Wix pitches Business Elite (US$159/mo) at multi-location restaurant groups (deeper reporting and support). — [Onplattr](https://onplattr.com/blog/wix-for-restaurants)
- Norwegian multi-location patterns: Cutters (96 salons, 2021), Dr.Dropin (per-city clinic pages with local transport/parking/hours), club umbrella structures with 50,000+ local branches across member organisations; Frivillighet Norge wants bulk registration of local branches and a heavy discount on registry fees for national orgs. — [NHO](https://nho.no/om-nho/artikler/nho-styrker-posisjonen-som-norges-storste-fellesskap-for-bedrifter), [Dr.Dropin Bergen](https://drdropin.no/fysikalsk/klinikker/bergen), [EACEA](https://national-policies.eacea.ec.europa.eu/youthwiki/chapters/norway/22-administration-and-governance-of-youth-volunteering), [Frivillighet Norge](https://frivillighetnorge.no/politikk/bredere-bruk-av-frivillighetsregisteret)

### Inferences
- Platform data model: Organisation (brand tokens, global content, legal) -> Location (NAP, hours, services subset, staff subset, booking link/ID, GBP ID, reviews) -> Page (location landing page, generated from a template but forced to include unique fields; block publishing if uniqueness fields are empty).

## 5. Design: avoiding "every site looks the same" (design systems, brand extraction from logo, AI style variation)

### Takeaway
Current builders reduce sameness mainly via curated style "directions" + font/colour palettes (Squarespace Blueprint), prompt-to-site generation with a chat agent (Wix Harmony/Aria), URL/screenshot/Figma-to-style (Readdy) and theme-level tokens (Squarespace Site Themes). - Readdy: accepts screenshot, branding mock or Figma frame, or a pasted URL, to mirror look/structure; vibe prompts ("minimal white layout", "luxury black and gold", "colorful artist theme"). — [Readdy](https://readdy.ai)

### Inferences
- A design-token system (colour roles, type scale, radius, spacing, imagery treatment, motion) with 20-40 curated "directions" per vertical, then logo-derived palette (extract dominant colours, check contrast to WCAG AA, derive neutrals) and font pairing from a vetted list, gives variety while guaranteeing accessibility and Core Web Vitals.

- Guardrails against the failure modes seen in Blueprint tests: contrast check, alt-text required, animations off by default or reduced-motion aware, block publish on placeholder content, forbid AI-invented claims/awards/reviews.

### Gaps
- No primary documentation found on how Wix, Squarespace, Durable or Hostinger extract brand colours/fonts from an uploaded logo or existing site; claims are secondary or generic.
- No quantitative data on perceived "sameness" or on conversion lift from distinctive design.

## 6. Overlooked factors: multilingual (bokmål/nynorsk/English/Sámi), accessibility, speed, local SEO/GBP, reviews, image rights, claims and payments

### Takeaway
Accessibility is a live legal duty for private Norwegian sites aimed at the public (WCAG 2.0 A/AA under the IKT-forskrift, enforced by Uutilsynet with tvangsmulkt), with the EU Accessibility Act (EU since 28 June 2025) not yet incorporated in Norwegian law per two sources. Product-specific Norwegian-language duties apply (toys). Nynorsk, Sámi and image-rights specifics are thinly sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Accessibility law: Likestillings- og diskrimineringsloven §§17-18 and the IKT-forskrift require universal design of ICT; private sector standard WCAG 2.0 A and AA (with exemption for audio description); public sector WCAG 2.1; accessibility statement mandatory only for public bodies; supervised by Uutilsynet (Digdir): notice at least 14 days before test, preliminary report, binding order, deadline, tvangsmulkt if not fixed; users can also complain to Diskrimineringsnemnda. — [Internkontrollportalen 2026-04-12](https://internkontrollportalen.no/universell-utforming-av-ikt-losninger), [Webtoget 2026-09-25](https://webtoget.no/artikler/universell-utforming-nettside), [Fokus Digital 2026-07-08](https://fokusdigital.no/artikler/universell-utforming-nettside)
- Enforcement stats: of 90 sites/apps with breaches, 68 were fixed and 22 got tvangsmulkt decisions (2 already running). — [Fokus Digital citing Uutilsynet](https://fokusdigital.no/artikler/universell-utforming-nettside)
- EAA in force in EU since 28 June 2025 (covers e-commerce, banking, e-books, passenger transport sites); as of May 2026 (and Sept 2026 per Webtoget) not yet in force in Norway because EEA incorporation is delayed; the regulation text for private firms still says WCAG 2.0 (last changed 2023). One webshop vendor blog claims EAA applies to all EEA webshops already — conflicting and less reliable. — [Internkontrollportalen](https://internkontrollportalen.no/universell-utforming-av-ikt-losninger), [Webtoget](https://webtoget.no/artikler/universell-utforming-nettside); contradicted by [Nettpilot 2026-03-11](https://nettpilot.no/universell-utforming-for-nettbutikker-i-2026-lovkrav-og-strategiske-fordeler)
- The 35 UU requirements for websites cover alt text, video captions/transcripts, headings/lists/tables structure, page titles, etc. — [Nettsmed 2026-10-02](https://nettsmed.no/universell-utforming-nettside)
- Language: Toys require all labelling/safety info in Norwegian; consumer products generally need warnings in a language Norwegian users reasonably understand (Norwegian or understandable Scandinavian). — [DSB Leketøy](https://dsb.no/produkter-og-forbrukertjenester/produkter/leketoy), [DSB internkontroll guide](https://dsb.no/produkter-og-forbrukertjenester/veiledning/veileder-i-internkontroll-for-importorer-og-produsenter-av-forbrukerprodukter)

### Inferences
- Treat WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA as the design target (exceeds the legal WCAG 2.0 minimum and anticipates EAA); automate contrast, alt text, heading order, form labels and keyboard focus checks in the editor and block publish on failures; make it a marketing point because Uutilsynet enforcement is real but targeted.
- Languages: ship bokmål as default, nynorsk as a first-class locale (UI + email templates + forms; many municipalities/NRK-style audiences and some clubs prefer it), English for tourism/restaurants, and an optional Sámi-ready locale model (hreflang `se`, special characters in fonts: Á Č Đ Ŋ Š Ŧ Ž) — the Sámi requirement for private firms was not found, so treat it as an optional pack for businesses in Sámi areas/duodji/tourism (inference, no source).
- Image rights: require provenance metadata per image (own/stock/AI), model-release flag for people photos, credit field for photographers, and BONO/Kopinor-aware licence note for artists' works; AI-generated people should be disabled by default for staff/team sections.
- Local SEO: auto-generate LocalBusiness/subtype schema, opening hours incl. holidays, sameAs links, per-location GBP link, review snippets only when real; sync NAP with GBP.

### Gaps
- No primary Norwegian source found on Sámi language obligations for private businesses' websites, or on bokmål/nynorsk obligations for private webshops (the Språkrådet/Språklova duties apply to public bodies; private rules not confirmed here).
- No source retrieved on Norwegian image-rights/personal-image rules (åndsverkloven, personopplysningsloven for photos of customers/staff, photographers' credit), Forbrukertilsynet guidance on fake/purchased reviews, or Datatilsynet/ekom-law cookie rules (the user's own regelverksradar should be consulted before claims).
- No Norwegian page-speed benchmark data for builder sites.
- Whether Norway has incorporated the EAA after Sept 2026 was not confirmed; recheck before making legal claims.
