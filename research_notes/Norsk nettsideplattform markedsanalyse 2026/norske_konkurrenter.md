# Norwegian (and Nordic) website and webshop platform competitors, as of October 2026

Research date: 31 Oct 2026. All prices NOK, excl. VAT unless stated. Most price and traction data comes from vendors' own pages or from agency blogs that sell the same services, so treat them as claims. Where sources conflict this is stated. Publication dates are given where the page showed one; "acquired" dates are the search engine's crawl date, not a publication date.

## 1. Who would a new Norwegian website/webshop platform compete with locally: what do they charge, offer, and how much traction do they have?

### Takeaway
The Norwegian webshop-platform niche is small and concentrated: Mystore (Visma-owned, NOK 95M revenue, ~1,600 shops claimed) is the only local SaaS of scale, 24Nettbutikk and Quickbutik are roughly 300-550 shops each in third-party scans, and Shopify (~9-10k stores) plus WooCommerce (~7-8k) hold ~65% of verified Norwegian stores. The "website, not shop" end is fragmented between hosting companies' bundled builders (Domeneshop, Webhuset, Uniweb, ProISP, One.com), global builders (Wix, Squarespace), and a wave of small-agency subscription offers at roughly 400-960 kr/month.

### Inferences
- Mystore's own customer count (1,600) is ~60% above Store Leads' live count (1,017), so Norwegian shop-count claims by SaaS vendors should be treated as upper bounds; roughly 300-550 Norwegian shops each for 24Nettbutikk and Quickbutik are the independently verified scale of "local SaaS".
- Mystore is priced at 1,200-2,000 kr/mnd for Start/Smart, which is 3-4x Shopify Basic (389), while the other local SaaS (Komplett Nettbutikk from 499, Quickbutik from 199-319, Nettify 499) sit near or below Shopify. This leaves a price gap but also shows where local vendors cannot compete on fixed fees.
- Mystore's thin equity (1.0M on 95M revenue) and its move onto the Acendy brand suggest the largest local player is being folded into a Visma pan-European product; a Norwegian independent could position against that.
- Hosting companies treat the site builder as a hosting feature (39-99 kr/mnd), so any new builder priced above ~400 kr/mnd is competing with agency-style "ferdig nettside" subscriptions rather than with these bundles.

## 2. What do agencies and freelancers charge in Norway in 2025/2026 for a one-page site, a simple business site, a webshop, and maintenance?

### Takeaway
Published-price agencies cluster at about 10,000-25,000 kr for a template-based multi-page site (median of cheapest packages 19,000 kr ex VAT, 17 agencies, 9 Oct 2026) while "typical business site" guides say 30,000-100,000 kr; simple webshops are 15,000-60,000 kr at the low end and 60,000-150,000 kr for a "standard" Shopify/WooCommerce build. Maintenance is typically 300-3,000 kr/mnd; hourly rates are 900-1,800 kr (agency) and 600-1,200 kr (freelancer).

### Inferences
- Nearly every source is an agency or platform selling the service, and the ranges are not statistics; the lower quartile (about 10,000-15,000 kr for a template site, 15,000-30,000 for a simple shop) is best treated as the real price pressure from small agencies and freelancers.
- A "simple business site" has two clearly different price points: ~10,000-25,000 (template, published pricing) and 30,000-100,000 (custom design, typical of guide articles). Maintenance of 500-1,500 kr/mnd is the commonly bundled attach.
- Three-year cost comparisons favour the subscription (e.g. PC113: 19,000 + 500/mnd = 37,000 over three years vs. 799/mnd = ~29,000), which explains the current subscription push.

### Gaps
- No independent survey (SSB, Virke, Brønnøysund) of agency pricing; "no official statistics" is stated by Mementor for marketing agencies.
- No reliable 2025 vs 2026 trend data; most sources have no year-on-year comparison.
- Freelancer-specific hourly rate data for web development is thin (marketing-oriented sources used).

## 3. What do Norwegian "nettside på abonnement" offers cost, and what are the binding, ownership, and cancellation terms?

### Takeaway
Subscription offers cluster at 400-960 kr/mnd (+ often 0-9,000 kr setup), with terms ranging from no binding (Smartbyrå, Nettify, Wild Supply, bedriftside.no) to 12 months (DigitalData). The key differentiator is ownership: most operate the site on their own stack, so the site goes offline on cancellation, while domain is usually promised in the customer's name.

### Cited Findings
- Smartbyrå (MedMalin AS, org.nr 936 934 781): 799 kr/mnd + VAT, no setup fee, no binding, month-to-month, shop included. On cancellation "siden tas av nett", domain registered in the company's name at Norid stays with you, texts and images are yours. Extras: .no domain 49 kr/mnd, e-mail 99 kr/mnd per account. Nettsmed saw Smartbyrå at 299 kr/mnd in September and 799 in October 2026. — [smartbyra.no (2026-10-23)](https://smartbyra.no); [Nettsmed](https://nettsmed.no/webbyra-priser/)
- Nettify: Webshop 1,999 setup + 499 kr/mnd, up to 80 products, no binding, hosting, domain, Vipps, Posten/Bring included; Nettsidebygger (AI, build yourself) 399 kr/mnd with free .no domain; "Nettify Levert" (finished site, no shop) 800 kr/mnd with no setup fee. — [Nettify nettbutikk-pris, 28 Jul 2026](https://nettify.no/nettbutikk-pris)
- Uniweb: 559 / 759 / 959 kr/mnd + 6,999 / 7,999 / 8,999 setup, 5 / 7 / 10 pages, 2 / 5 / 12 changes per year, delivery within 30 days. Binding period not retrieved. — [Uniweb](https://uniweb.no/nettside/hva-koster-en-nettside)
- Wild Supply Digital: one-pager 3,990 setup + 499 kr/mnd; standard (up to 6 pages) 5,990 + 499; advanced from 15,000 + from 999 kr/mnd; 1-3 support hours/month; "ingen binding". — [wildsupply.no](https://wildsupply.no)
- bedriftside.no: 3,999 once + 399 kr/mnd, no binding, one month's notice. Owner of domain, texts, images, Google Business profile is the customer, but "the technical solution (the code) is owned and operated by us"; site is active only while the drift agreement runs. — [bedriftside.no](https://bedriftside.no)
- DigitalData: no setup, 12 months binding, then 1 month notice; domain is the customer's. Monthly price not retrieved. — [digitaldata.no](https://digitaldata.no/nettside)
- dinnettside.no: 1,499 kr/mnd year 1, 999 from year 2, unlimited changes; terms not retrieved. — [dinnettside.no](https://dinnettside.no/fagstoff/nettside-pris)
- NETN, Digstra, Larsen Commerce position against subscriptions: NETN has one month notice on running agreements; Digstra "du eier alt, ingen binding eller exit-gebyrer"; Larsen sells one-page site for 2,999 once (list 5,999) + 499 kr/yr and says typical subscriptions are 500-800 kr/mnd with "you never own the site; stop paying and it disappears" (comparison figures are the seller's own). — [NETN](https://netn.no/priser); [Digstra](https://digstra.no/tjenester/nynettsidebedrift); [Larsen Commerce](https://larsencommerce.no)
- Independent-ish commentary: "Subscription / 'website as a service'... Low entry cost, but you rarely own the solution yourself. If you terminate the agreement, you often lose the entire website." — [ur.solutions, Jun 2026](https://ursolutions.no/blog/hva-koster-en-nettside); PXL: "Fast lavpris med abonnement. Da eier du ikke nettsiden." — [PXL](https://pxl.no/tjenester/wordpress/pris). WebPlug requires a maintenance agreement (from 490 kr/mnd) to buy the site (Nettsmed table). Nettsmed lists "who owns site, domain and code on termination?" as a question to ask any provider. — [Nettsmed](https://nettsmed.no/webbyra-priser/)
- Regulatory context: Norwegian electronic-communications rules (mobile/broadband) cap binding at 12 months, but I found no equivalent rule for B2B website subscriptions. — [Nkom](https://nkom.no/telefoni-og-telefonnummer/informasjon-om-telefoni-for-sluttbruker/abonnement) (telecom only)

### Inferences
- The market has moved to "no binding, domain in your name" as the headline promise in 2026 (6 of 8 offers above), which means a competitor offering the same is table stakes and ownership of the code/site export is the open differentiator.
- Lock-in is therefore economic rather than contractual: the site is hosted on the vendor's stack and disappears on cancellation.
- Price instability (Smartbyrå 299 to 799 within weeks) and many near-identical, newly launched sellers suggest low barriers to entry and AI-assisted production.

### Gaps
- Uniweb binding terms; DigitalData monthly price; whether any of these vendors offer a code export on cancellation.
- No customer counts or financials for any of the subscription sellers.
- Terms of the 2023-2026 wave were taken from the sellers' own marketing pages, not contracts.

## 4. Platform adoption among Norwegian webshops: Shopify vs WooCommerce vs Mystore vs others

### Takeaway
Two independent scanners agree: Shopify is the largest platform for Norwegian webshops (about 9,300-9,900 stores, ~35% of verified stores), WooCommerce second (~29-32%, ~7,700-7,900), then custom builds (~19-20%). Local SaaS platforms are small (24Nettbutikk 2%, Quickbutik 1%, Mystore not in the top 10 of the ShopRank list).

### Cited Findings
- ShopRank, 27 Oct 2026, 26,384 verified active Norwegian stores with physical delivery: Shopify 9,274 (35.2%), WooCommerce 7,713 (29.2%), Custom 5,112 (19.4%), Wix 866 (3.3%), 24Nettbutikk 537 (2.0%), Magento 516, Squarespace 387, Quickbutik 280, PrestaShop 264. Among 70,478 domains with any e-commerce signal: WooCommerce 36.2%, Shopify 22.0%, Wix 10.7%, Squarespace 6.4%. — [ShopRank Norway](https://shoprank.com/countries/norway)
- ShopRank earlier snapshot (18 May 2026): Shopify 35.4%, WooCommerce 31.6%, Custom 20.3%; "Mystore.no, 24nettbutikk" appears as two combined detections (326 and 292 stores, 1.3% and 1.2%), so Mystore and 24Nettbutikk were not separated then. — [ShopRank, via search result](https://shoprank.com/countries/norway)
- Store Leads (15 May 2026): 9,928 live Shopify stores in Norway; growth +22% YoY and +1.3% QoQ in Q1 2026; series from 2,028 in Q1 2019 to 9,796 in Q1 2026. — [Store Leads Shopify NO](https://storeleads.app/reports/shopify/NO/top-stores). Quickbutik NO 324 (see section 1).
- BuiltWith-derived (older): Wix Stores 28%, Shopify 17%, Squarespace 16%, WooCommerce 15% (cited Mar 2023); earlier snapshot Shopify 17 / Squarespace add-to-cart 16 / WooCommerce 14 / Magento 11 / Mystore 6 percent (Nettpilot, 2019). A 2024 article says Shopify, Wix Stores, Squarespace and WooCommerce are the most popular on BuiltWith and Ecwid and Mystore have "drøye 2 prosent". BuiltWith counts "add to cart" widgets on builder sites so it overweights Wix/Squarespace. — [netthandel.no 2023-03-10](https://netthandel.no/e-handlere-forteller-disse-plattformene-bruker-vi-og-hvorfor); [netthandel.no 2024-04-26](https://netthandel.no/slik-velger-du-rett-plattform-gode-rad-til-deg-som-skal-starte-nettbutikk); [Nettpilot 2019-07-23](https://nettpilot.no/antall-woocommerce-nettbutikker-i-norge)
- Survey of 21 larger Norwegian shops (netthandel.no, Mar 2023): 7 on Shopify, 3 WooCommerce, 2 Quickbutik, 2 own, others Norce, Magento, Optimizely, Sanity, Multicase. Not representative, mostly bigger shops. — [netthandel.no](https://netthandel.no/e-handlere-forteller-disse-plattformene-bruker-vi-og-hvorfor)
- Some vendor/agency pieces give Mystore "~10% share in 2023 (BuiltWith)" and 24Nettbutikk "~10%" — these contradict the numbers above and no BuiltWith page was available to check. — [Webkonsepter, 2025-06-04](https://webkonsepter.no/starte-nettbutikk)

### Inferences
- The consistent signal across ShopRank and Store Leads is Shopify gaining ~20% a year while local SaaS stays flat (Quickbutik NO +6% YoY); the realistic addressable switching base for a new platform is the ~10,000 Shopify and ~7,700 WooCommerce merchants, not Mystore's customers.
- Webshop count by platform understates revenue weight: Mystore's 1,000-1,600 shops process ~NOK 4.5bn a year, i.e. larger average merchants than the Shopify long tail.

### Gaps
- No BuiltWith or Statista page loaded directly for 2026; BuiltWith country list could not be fetched.
- Mystore's own count is not separately reported in the October ShopRank list (probably merged with 24Nettbutikk or classed as "custom").
- No revenue-weighted market share for Norway.

## 5. What do Norwegian customers complain about with local providers and agencies?

### Takeaway
The evidence I could reach is thin and mostly from Trustpilot: slow or hard-to-reach support and expensive add-on services at Mystore, setup complexity at 24Nettbutikk, long contracts and penalty fees at Eniro, and structural issues (renewal price jumps, hidden extras, ownership on cancellation) described by agency blogs. I found no Kundeklageportalen, Forbrukerrådet, diskusjon.no or Reddit threads specifically about Norwegian web providers.

### Cited Findings
- Mystore on Trustpilot: 1-star "Alltid kø til kundeservice... stenger tidlig og har lunsjstengt... Kjøpt dyr wefix-pakke, og det har virkelig ikke hjulpet noe" (29 Oct 2025); 1-star about customers charged too little, not at all, or twice by the checkout (14 Apr 2024); 2-star about a theme costing "100.000" (Apr 2023, ambiguous); 5-star "God løsning og god kundeservice" (9 Jun 2026). Very few reviews on the profile. — [Trustpilot Mystore.no](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/mystore.no)
- Mystore support hours: free Norwegian support only Monday-Friday daytime, with 24/7 "critical support" for outages (2023 review site). — [nettbutikksguiden.no](https://nettbutikksguiden.no/mystore)
- 24Nettbutikk on Trustpilot: 5-star reviews (Jun and Oct 2025) praising support and built-in freight; a 1-star (Jun 2022) saying it is "a copy of WP/WooCommerce" with hundreds of settings and not quick to set up. Profile notes the company has not recently invited reviews. — [Trustpilot 24nettbutikk.no](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/24nettbutikk.no)
- Quickbutik's Trustpilot profile has no recent review invitations and I could not extract review text. — [Trustpilot Quickbutik](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/quickbutik.com)
- Eniro (Swedish Trustpilot page, 2026): complaints about trial-then-one-year-contract, annual invoice, penalty fee over EUR 600 for cancelling, "scam you for hundreds of kronor each month for years". Older SvD coverage (Jun 2015) on Eniro "bluff invoices" and telephone tactics. Norwegian-specific Gule Sider/Robin complaints not retrieved. — [Trustpilot Eniro](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/www.eniro.se); [SvD 2015](http://svd.se/sa-luras-du-av-eniros-telefonknep/om/turerna-i-eniro)
- Hidden costs and bait pricing (Uniweb's own guide): "99 kr/mnd" sites turning into 500 kr; hosting 150 extra; e-mail 50-100 kr; domain renewal 400-500 kr; support only in expensive plans. — [Uniweb billig-nettside, 2025-06-26](https://uniweb.no/nettside/billig-nettside)
- Intro-price renewals: Webhuset hosting roughly doubles at renewal (15 to 30, 45 to 120 kr/mnd). — [topsitehosters.com](https://topsitehosters.com/providers/webhuset)
- Agency contract practices flagged by Norwegian blogs: 12-month binding with auto-renewal called a "rødt flagg" (Innovena; marketing agencies), typical agency binding 6-12 months; hidden costs from per-hour changes (800-1,500 kr/h), required maintenance agreements, and "from" prices that exclude content. — [Innovena](https://innovena.no/byra/reklamebyraa-bergen); [WebMedia](https://webmedia.no); [dinnettside.no](https://dinnettside.no/fagstoff/nettside-pris); [Nettsmed](https://nettsmed.no/webbyra-priser/)
- Platform consolidation risk: Mijnwebwinkel customers moved to higher-priced Acendy (EUR 40+ vs 20-70) — a precedent for Mystore customers. — [codemaker-s.com](https://codemaker-s.com/en/platform/mijnwebwinkel)
- Domeneshop's terms state replies within 2 working days and that prices can be changed at any time at its discretion. — [Domeneshop terms](https://domene.shop/terms)
- Shopify cost complaints are framed as transaction fees and app costs (500-5,000 kr/mnd). — [Digitelle, 2026-01-27](https://digitelle.no/woocommerce-vs-shopify-hvilken-nettbutikklosning-bor-du-velge-i-norge)

### Inferences
- The recurring themes are (1) support reachability and cost of paid setup help, (2) ownership/exit on cancellation, (3) intro price vs. real price, (4) forced maintenance. These are the points on which a small provider with written support, transparent price and code export could differentiate; the competitor subscription sellers already advertise on 2 and 3.
- Mystore's per-transaction Vipps fee (3.99% on Start) is high relative to the 1.4-2.9% range quoted for payment fees generally, so fee transparency is a probable pain point, but no review in my sample says so.

### Gaps
- No threads found from Reddit r/norge, diskusjon.no, Facebook small-business groups, Kundeklageportalen or Forbrukerrådet about web providers.
- Trustpilot samples are tiny (Mystore, 24Nettbutikk show a handful of reviews each); Webhuset 4.6 and Uniweb 4.5 are from the vendors or aggregators and not independently checked.
- Complaints about freelancer-built sites (abandonment, no handover) were not found as sourced material.
